# AI Context
- Summary: Turns the graph into a road surface by extruding a cross-section along each segment's arc-length polyline, regenerated from the graph after every edit, with a flat disc covering each junction.
- Keywords: road mesh, extrusion, cross-section, regeneration from graph, disc junction, road width by type
- Use when: generating or regenerating road geometry, or tracking down a mesh that survived an edit to the graph.
- Skip when: the junction needs to look correct up close -- that is the trimmed-polygon item; or the road needs to follow relief -- that is the heightmap item.

# Problem
- The network is drawn as bare lines and has to become a surface before anything reads as a city.
- A junction leaves the ends of its incident segments visible as open edges, and something has to cover them.

# Scope
- In:
  - A road surface generated by extruding a cross-section along each segment's arc-length polyline.
  - Regeneration of the affected meshes after each edit, from the graph, with the mesh never edited directly.
  - A flat disc of road surface at each junction node, sized to the widest incident segment, covering the segment ends.
  - The road width coming from the segment's type rather than being fixed in the mesh code.
- Out:
  - Trimmed-polygon junctions, which the later item covers.
  - Curbs, lane markings, sidewalks and signals.
  - Any conformance to relief; the ground is flat here.

# Acceptance criteria
- AC1: Each segment renders as a road surface following its curve, at the width its type declares.
- AC2: Editing the graph regenerates the affected meshes and nothing else; no mesh is mutated outside regeneration.
- AC3: A junction shows no open segment edge from the play camera.
- AC4: Deleting or splitting a segment leaves no orphaned mesh behind.
